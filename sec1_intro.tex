\section{Introduction}

% Context
The integration of renewable energy sources into the power grid has driven the development of Virtual Power Plants (VPPs) to aggregate and manage distributed energy resources. Accurate forecasting of VPP dynamics---specifically electrical load, photovoltaic (PV) generation, and wind power generation---is critical for grid stability, economic dispatch, and operational risk management \cite{Ma2025,Cao2023,Qiu2024,Moreno2020,Wu2024}. Unlike single-variable forecasting, cooperative VPP management requires simultaneous, coherent predictions across these interrelated domains, where the physical constraints of each energy modality must be rigorously respected. For instance, PV generation is intrinsically bound by solar irradiance and cannot produce power during nighttime, while wind generation depends on a non-linear turbine power curve driven by localized wind speed. Reliable dispatch decisions hinge not only on minimizing raw statistical forecasting errors but also on ensuring that predictive models fundamentally comply with these immutable physical laws.

% Problem Statement
Recent advancements in deep learning have introduced powerful time-series forecasting architectures, yet their application to VPP scheduling presents fundamental challenges \cite{Vle2025,Arabzadeh2025}. While deep neural networks excel at minimizing statistical error metrics, they operate as black-box predictors that remain oblivious to the underlying physical principles governing power systems. In the present study's benchmark evaluations, the strongest Transformer baseline achieves a boundary violation rate of 19.53\%, while recurrent architectures---despite their sequential inductive bias---still exhibit violation rates of 11.25\%. Consequently, pure data-driven frameworks frequently generate dimensionally accurate but physically impossible predictions, such as non-zero solar power during the night or negative wind generation, which severely undermines operational safety and operator trust. Furthermore, the opaque nature of these architectures limits interpretability, making it exceedingly difficult to causally attribute anomalous predictions to specific meteorological inputs. Bridging the gap between the high predictive capacity of deep learning and the rigorous physical compliance required by coupled power systems remains a critical challenge, necessitating an approach that simultaneously ensures accuracy, causal interpretability, and robust physical adherence. While heuristic post-processing---such as hard zero-clipping at physical boundaries---can retroactively enforce elementary compliance, it inherently introduces non-differentiable dead zones that disrupt end-to-end optimization. More critically, such deterministic truncation fails to dynamically capture the state-dependent constraints of interconnected physical components, thus necessitating a framework that natively integrates differentiable physical priors upstream of the forecasting output.

% Contributions
This paper proposes PhysFormer, a physics-guided causal Transformer designed to occupy a superior position on the accuracy-interpretability-physical compliance Pareto frontier for VPP forecasting. The proposed architecture does not pursue absolute accuracy minimization alone; rather, it simultaneously satisfies criteria across three dimensions previously unachieved in tandem. In the first dimension of accuracy, the model achieves a mean squared error of 0.0127, within 5\% of the strongest black-box baseline. In the second dimension of interpretability, the framework provides quantifiable causal attribution yielding a gate-irradiance Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.816. In the third dimension of physical compliance, the boundary violation rate is reduced to 0.46\%, systematically lower than all evaluated neural baselines.

The primary contributions of this paper are threefold. First, a Physics-Guided Dual-Stream Architecture with Physics Activity Gating is proposed. This framework incorporates parallel Transformer and ExplicitPhysicalMapping streams, fused via multiplicative activity masks for PV and wind components. These masks suppress neural residual corrections when the physical model certifies device inactivity, reducing the boundary violation rate to 0.46\% without imposing hard downstream output constraints. Second, Physics-Guided Causal Coupling (PGCC) is introduced as a volatility-aware cross-attention mechanism with learnable irradiance and wind-speed thresholds. This mechanism produces a PV gating variable that correlates with solar irradiance at a Pearson coefficient of 0.816 across the test set, providing quantifiable causal interpretability absent in black-box baselines. Third, Mean Violation Severity (MVS) is formally identified as a necessary companion metric to the boundary violation rate, documenting a systemic evaluation bias against high-accuracy near-zero predictors. MVS reveals that models with lower BVR can conceal substantially larger per-violation amplitude: for instance, DLinear achieves the lowest baseline BVR of 7.03\% yet records an MVS of 0.0295\,MW---eight times larger than Informer's 0.0036\,MW---demonstrating that violation frequency alone cannot characterize operational risk. PhysFormer achieves a BVR of 0.46\% with an MVS of 0.0149\,MW; rather than purely claiming this as marginal fluctuation, this establishes that PhysFormer successfully breaks the dichotomy of low MVS necessitating high BVR (as seen in Informer), while maintaining a violation magnitude substantially lower than conservative baselines like DLinear.
