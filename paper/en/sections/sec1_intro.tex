\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}
The integration of renewable energy sources into the power grid has driven the development of Virtual Power Plants (VPPs) to aggregate and manage distributed energy resources. Accurate forecasting of VPP dynamics---specifically electrical load, photovoltaic (PV) generation, and wind power generation---is critical for grid stability, economic dispatch, and operational risk management \cite{Ma2025,Cao2023,Qiu2024,Moreno2020,Wu2024}. Unlike single-variable forecasting, cooperative VPP management requires simultaneous, coherent predictions across these interrelated domains, where the physical constraints of each energy modality must be rigorously respected. For instance, PV generation is intrinsically bound by solar irradiance and cannot produce power during nighttime, while wind generation depends on a non-linear turbine power curve driven by localized wind speed. 

Historically, early approaches to VPP forecasting relied on statistical extrapolation. As distributed resources increased, recurrent neural networks and Transformer-based frameworks became widely used for sequential modeling, especially when long-range temporal dependencies and multi-source interactions must be captured \cite{Zhou2021informer,Wu2021autoformer,Nie2023patchtst,Zeng2023}. Recent application-oriented studies have continued to adapt these architectures to load forecasting and VPP scheduling scenarios \cite{Qiu2024,Ashraf2025,Du2025,Li2025MetaInformer}. In these settings, low statistical error is important, but so is maintaining predictions that remain consistent with basic physical operating conditions.

\subsection{Challenges of Data-Driven VPP Forecasting}
Recent advances in deep learning have improved time-series forecasting accuracy, yet their application to VPP prediction still involves several practical challenges \cite{Vle2025,Arabzadeh2025}:

\textbf{1) Output feasibility and activation consistency:} Purely data-driven predictors can achieve competitive error metrics while still producing physically inconsistent outputs, such as non-zero solar generation at night or negative renewable power. In VPP applications, these violations may be small in magnitude, but they complicate downstream interpretation and operational use.

\textbf{2) Limits of post-processing and static penalties:} Post-hoc clipping is a practical remedy for deployment, but it does not guide the model during training. Conversely, physics-informed penalties can regularize optimization, yet fixed constraints may not be sufficient to reflect weather-driven activation changes in PV and wind generation \cite{Raissi2019,Li2026,Asinyo2025}. A useful forecasting architecture should therefore balance train-time guidance with flexible residual modeling.

\textbf{3) Need for interpretable weather-response behavior:} For deployment-oriented forecasting, it is helpful to understand whether activation patterns in the model remain aligned with observable weather cues. Existing interpretability tools are informative, but they do not necessarily encode these relationships into the model structure itself \cite{Vle2025,Lovo2025,Arabzadeh2025}.

\subsection{Overview of This Work}
To address these issues, this paper presents \textbf{PhysFormer}, a physics-guided Transformer for physically consistent VPP forecasting. The proposed framework combines an explicit physical mapping stream, a bounded residual output parameterization, a physics-guided gating and alignment module (PGCC), and curriculum-based constraint training. In this work, PGCC is used as a guidance and alignment mechanism rather than as a causal discovery tool.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item We propose a \textbf{Bounded Physical Act-Residual (BPAR)} output mechanism that combines theory-based baselines with data-driven residual corrections, reducing non-physical predictions while preserving differentiable training dynamics.
    
    \item We introduce a \textbf{Physics-Guided Gating and Alignment} module (PGCC) that injects weather-dependent priors into PV and wind activation, improving the interpretability of the model's weather-response behavior through supervised alignment.
\end{enumerate}

In addition, we adopt a \textbf{Physics-Constrained Curriculum Training} strategy to stabilize multi-objective optimization. Experiments on a real 3-year VPP dataset show that PhysFormer reaches zero boundary violations while remaining close in MSE to strong Transformer baselines, illustrating a practical trade-off between forecasting accuracy, physical consistency, and engineering interpretability.
