\begin{abstract}
	Virtual power plant (VPP) forecasting must jointly predict load, photovoltaic (PV), and wind power while avoiding non-physical outputs such as negative generation or nighttime solar power. Simple post-processing can remove some violations at deployment, but it does not constrain the training dynamics or clarify how weather priors influence model behavior. This paper presents PhysFormer, a physics-guided Transformer for physically consistent VPP forecasting. The framework combines an explicit physical mapping stream, a bounded residual output mechanism, a physics-guided gating and alignment module, and curriculum-based constraint training. The bounded residual design keeps predictions non-negative under the adopted normalization setting, while the gating and regularization terms align PV and wind activation with exogenous weather cues. Experiments on a real-world 3-year VPP dataset show that PhysFormer achieves a boundary violation rate of 0.00\% and remains close in MSE to strong Transformer baselines, with an MSE of 0.0128 compared with 0.0121 and 0.0122 for Informer and iTransformer, respectively. These results indicate that the proposed framework provides a practical trade-off among forecasting accuracy, physical consistency, and interpretable weather-response behavior for joint VPP component prediction.
\end{abstract}
